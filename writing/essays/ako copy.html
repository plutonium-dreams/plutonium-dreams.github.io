Streets of Bacolod (DRAFT)

by kent nico

28/6/2024

Between the streets of Bacolod and its sister city, Iloilo, someone can easily point out a few contrasting characteristics. A noticeable difference is that the roads of Bacolod are noticeably wider compared to Iloilo’s—although that doesn’t necessarily translate to spaciousness.

As a pedestrian, Bacolod’s most visible streets often lack proper infrastructure. A stroll around the city’s roads would easily make clear to someone that sidewalks are only concentrated areas such as the downtown and capitol; roads such as BS Aquino Drive are lacking in pedestrian amenities that many people, including students, regularly use. Not to mention, these concerns are further exacerbated during the night. Many streets are lacking or have dwindling street lamps, amplifying the danger faced by night workers.

Motorists are not exempt from the failures of Bacolod’s roads; the city’s traffic lights often malfunction, and traffic is a rising issue that the city continues to face as its population grows. Vehicle owners, however, face a better situation compared to the regular pedestrian. Despite being part of a privileged minority, motorists are favored by current and future developments in the city, with urban sprawl and government disinterest in proper public transportation on the rise. This would be a really interesting topic to unpack, and I have a lot to say regarding the haphazard city planning of Bacolod in the current years that show little to no regard for the pedestrian; however, I must ground myself on the purpose of this essay: to walk you through Bacolod’s streets

Bacolod City is crossed by the national highway with Lacson and Araneta streets. Both roads join at their intersections with Luzuriaga St. in the city center. Towards the east, Burgos Avenue and Alijis Road stretch toward the mountains, reaching Barangay Alangilan and the Municipality of Murcia, respectively.

Observation of Lacson Street’s architecture is akin to performing dendrochronology on a tree. One can notice, by observing and through logical conclusion, that the buildings standing along this road tend to be younger and more contemporary as one goes further away from the city center. Of course, this is to be expected because cities expand and develop, but this contrasting phenomenon is the clearest along Lacson relative to the other streets in Bacolod.

The section of Lacson Street around the city center is lined with withering buildings from the latter half of the past century with contemporary structures such as the Negros First Cyber Centre near surviving wooden houses of old. Moving north, the section of the street from the Provincial Hospital up to the Provincial Lagoon houses modernist government buildings such as the SSS. In front of Lagoon are fast-food restaurants that many Bacolodnons have fond memories of. Go further down the road and one arrives at the Lacson Tourism Strip—a section filled with commercial spaces, bars, and hotels that are contemporary in style (with glass walls and everything). From this point onwards, the buildings only get newer, more “hippy”, and higher. In fact, the tallest buildings in Bacolod tower over the section of Lacson just after Bangga Ramos and up to the very end of the road at the Northbound Terminal.

Burgos Street also has a similar gradient to Lacson street where half a century old buildings can be found near the city center and newer developments can be found near the block of the Bacolod City Government Center. Moving past the intersection with Circumferential road, however, one will start to see subdivisions and undeveloped farmlands which is unlike Lacson’s rapidly developing extremities.
